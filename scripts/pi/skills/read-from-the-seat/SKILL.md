# Does it read from the learner's seat?

The test: a child sees the scene once, without being told, and gets the word. Judge from the learner's view
(overview.jpg, and the orbit "front" tile), not the side or top views.

## Common reasons a scene doesn't read
- Flat things look edge-on. The learner looks almost level, so a table top, board, pond, map or ground is a thin line.
  Tilt its top toward the viewer (often 25-35 degrees about x). A `faces` goal measures this.
- The key thing is too small. The thing the word is about must be big and near the kanji. A `big` goal measures
  it as % of the kanji's size on screen.
- Too wide. Only about 1u of room shows right of a two-glyph word; things farther out leave the picture.
- Hidden. A person under 85% visible, or the key thing behind a person, the label or another prop. Move the blocker
  aside or the thing forward (+z).
- Two things overlapping into one blob (two rings, two dots inside a cloud) read as one shape. Keep a gap.

## What you may do
Fix numbers the plan gave you (a position, size, tilt) if the goals still pass. A change of design (new piece,
different action) is Opus's: describe it in the report, with what you saw from the seat.
