# Plan 004: 背 "height; back" (card 80cc), scene `q-back-to-back`, a plan in words

This plan is different: it says **what** the scene must show, not the code. You write the code, render, look, fix, and
repeat until it looks right. Take your time; up to **five** render-and-fix rounds are allowed in this plan.

## Where the code is

- The scene: `function backs(ctx, spec, stage)` in `src/effects/vignettes/q-music2.js` (the `// ---- 背 ----` block).
  Read it first; keep its structure (the `step(t)` function, the `timeline` beats, `pop`, the wave at the end).
- Its catalog line: `'q-back-to-back'` in `src/effects/vcat-q4c.js`. The `+ 3` there is the number of meshes the scene
  itself adds (here: the chart, the bar, the label). If you add a mesh, raise that number to match; the cost check tells
  you the right number.
- Change only those two places. For examples of the helpers, look at `exercise()` in `src/effects/vignettes/q-feel.js`
  (a grass patch built with `solidProp` + `G.cyl`) and the `room()` function right below `backs()` (a wooden floor built

- Units: `u` is the height of the kanji. `floor` is the bottom of the kanji: everything stands on it. `+z` is toward the
  viewer.

## What is wrong now (Opus's review)

1. **No ground.** The two children and the chart float in the sky. There must be a floor under them.
2. **The chart is a paper-thin card.** From behind and from the side it is a flat blue sheet. It must look like a solid
   board standing on the floor.
3. **The children overlap.** From the front, one child stands in front of the other, so you can't see that they stand
   back to back. Each child must be seen in clean side view (profile), one facing left and one facing right, their backs
   touching, both at the same depth and both against the chart.
4. **The height difference is too small** to notice at a glance.

## What the scene must show

A height chart (せいくらべ: comparing heights) standing on a floor to the right of the kanji:

- **Floor:** a wooden floor slab (colour like `room()`'s floor), about 1.0u wide and 0.5u deep, thin, its top exactly at
  `floor`. The chart and both children stand on it; the feet touch its top (not floating, not sunk).
- **Chart:** a board about 0.05u thick standing on the floor at the back of the slab, the same blue / green / ticks front
  as now, with a wooden back and edges so it looks solid from every side.
- **The children:** back to back, in front of the chart and close to it, both in pure profile (the tall one faces left,
  toward the kanji; the short one faces right), at the same depth, backs touching or almost touching, nobody's body
  passing through the other or through the chart. Make the tall one clearly taller: about `KID + 0.35`, and the short
  one about `KID + 0.05`.
- **The bar:** the wooden bar slides down the chart and comes to rest **on top of the tall child's head** (touching the
  hair, not floating above it, not sunk into it). It stays there while the short one goes up on tiptoe, then lifts away.
- **Label:** せいくらべ pops up beside the bar as now; it must not cover a face.
- At the end both turn to face us and wave, as now.

## Checks

The usual ones from the rules (`node --check` on both files, `npm test`, the cost check with `"^q-back-to-back$"`), and
two renders after each change:

- the whole scene: `node scripts/orbit.mjs 80cc --times 1.5,3.0,5.0 --dist 0.9 --out plans/qwen/renders/004-sei.jpg`
- close up: `node scripts/orbit.mjs 80cc --times 1.5,3.0 --dist 0.45 --at 0.5,0.3,0 --out plans/qwen/renders/004-sei-close.jpg`
  (if the children are not in the middle of the close view, change the `--at` x and y until they are)

What to look at, one question at a time, in every view:

1. Is there a floor under everything? Do all four feet touch its top?
2. From the **front**: can you see both children completely side by side, one facing left and one facing right? Or does
   one hide part of the other? (Hiding is a fault.)
3. From the **right side** and **back-left**: is the chart a thick board? Is any body passing through the chart or
   through the other child?
4. At t+1.5 and t+3.0: is the bar touching the top of the tall child's head? Look closely at the gap between the bar
   and the hair in the close render. A visible gap of sky is a fault; the bar inside the hair is a fault.
5. Is the height difference obvious?

Fix the first fault you find, re-render, ask the questions again. In the report, list each round: what you saw, what
you changed.
